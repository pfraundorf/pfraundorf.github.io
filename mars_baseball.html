Mars Baseball at Jezero

Jezero Dome · Perseverance landing site, 18.44°N 77.45°E · Jezero crater, Mars

A pressurised dome on the floor of Jezero crater, near where Perseverance landed, with the delta 2 km to the west and the crater rim on the horizon. Tonight the Arcadia Icebreakers visit the Jezero Deltas, in an ordinary-sized ballpark: 100 m down the lines, 122 m to centre, with an 8-foot wall.

This is P. Fraundorf's same-stadium recipe on Mars, where gravity is 0.38 of Earth's. The ball has 2.64× a baseball's mass (382 g, 13.5 oz), so it weighs what a baseball weighs on Earth. Players put the same work into every pitch, swing and throw, so the ball carries Earth energies along Earth arcs, just more slowly:

Leaping catches over the 2.4 m wall are routine here. A fielder can't be carried over it, though: at the top of a 1.3 m leap their feet are still a metre below the top of the wall. The dome holds 60 kPa of air with 40% oxygen, as many habitat studies propose, so the oxygen is like sea level on Earth. That air pushes up on the 340 m roof with 5.4 billion newtons. Holding that down with regolith would take a layer 10 m deep, so the roof is a cable net anchored into the ground. The thinner air means drag is only about a quarter as strong, relative to the ball's weight, as on Earth, so long drives carry farther.
