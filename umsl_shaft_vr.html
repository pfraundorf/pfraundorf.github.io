Down the shaft beneath UMSL's North Campus quad

An imaginary 3,900-ft shaft dug into the lawn of the University of Missouri–St. Louis North Campus quadrangle, lined with a ladder, lit only by your headlamp. The walls show the strata a geologist would expect here: wind-blown Ice Age silt, Pennsylvanian "coal measures", Mississippian limestones full of crinoids and chert, Ordovician and Cambrian carbonates, and Precambrian granite at the bottom.

Desktop: drag to look · S/↓ climb down · W/↑ climb up · hold Shift for ×10 · 123 speed · Space auto-descend · N/P next/previous layer · L headlamp · click a layer in the list to jump.

VR headset: thumbstick down/up climbs · hold grip for speed (both grips = express) · A/X next layer · B/Y previous layer · trigger toggles the headlamp · right stick left/right snap-turns. A panel on your left shows depth and the current layer.

Illustrative composite: thicknesses and depths are best estimates from regional maps and reports, not a borehole log for this spot. Uncertainty grows with depth (±100–300 ft below ~1,000 ft).
