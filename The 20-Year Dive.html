Last mail call

A station hovers above the horizon. The diver falls past it like a raindrop and has a birthday every wristwatch year. One birthday falls on the day she crosses the horizon, and the 20th coincides with the crunch. For each birthday, how late can the station send something and still have it arrive on time? Station clock times are years after the diver passes the station. A negative launch time means the package has to go out before she passes. It then waits ahead of her, and she catches up with it. "Package hits at" is the speed of the package relative to the diver when it reaches her. "Msg Doppler" is the received frequency divided by the sent frequency. A message reaching her at 0.2 also plays at 0.2 speed, so she sees home in slow motion.
What is being computed, and what is assumed

The black hole. Non-spinning (Schwarzschild). Its mass is chosen so that a raindrop takes exactly 20 years of wristwatch time to fall from the horizon to the centre: (4/3)·GM/c³ = 20 yr. That is about 9.6×1013 solar masses. The horizon radius is 30 light-years, which is larger than any black hole yet observed. Tides are negligible until the final second.

The diver is a raindrop, dropped from rest far away, as in Taylor, Wheeler and Bertschinger's Exploring Black Holes (chapters 7 and 12). Her local frame is the rain frame. In rain (Gullstrand–Painlevé) coordinates, ds² = −dT² + (dr + β dT)² + r²dΩ² with β = √(2M/r).

The view. Each viewing direction is traced backward as a light ray using the exact Schwarzschild orbit equation, giving the true sky position it came from. Stars are mapped the other way: each star image is placed by inverting that table, which gives the primary image and the secondary images that go around the hole. Image brightness includes lensing magnification and the exact Doppler-plus-gravitational shift, D = 1/(1+β cos α). Every star is treated as a blackbody at its catalog colour temperature. A shifted blackbody is still a blackbody, so the shifted colour and visual brightness are exact for that model. Stars are the real sky (AT-HYG v3.2, brighter than magnitude 8), placed infinitely far away with Orion's belt straight "up". The glow is a smoothed map of fainter catalog stars, a stand-in for the Milky Way. In reality a black hole this large would contain more than our neighbourhood of stars, so the sky here is a familiar backdrop, not a prediction.

Black regions. Outside the horizon, the black disc ahead is the hole's shadow. Inside, light from some directions has negative Killing energy. That light cannot have come from our sky: in an astrophysical black hole it comes from the collapsed star's surface, infinitely redshifted, so it is drawn black.

Mail call. The station hovers at fixed r and its clock runs at √(1−2M/r). Light-speed messages travel along ingoing light rays. Packages are launched inward at the chosen speed and then fall freely along radial geodesics. Each "last" launch is the latest station time whose message or package meets the diver at the stated birthday. Because the geometry does not change with time, the travel time from the station to any given r is the same whenever you launch.

Checks. The ray tracer was checked against an independent Python calculation (direct integrals and a separate ODE solver) to better than 10⁻⁴ rad wherever a star image can appear. The deadlines were checked the same way.
